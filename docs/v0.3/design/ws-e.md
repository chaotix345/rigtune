# WS-E (SPEC 3f, 8): benchmark design notes

Date: 2026-09-26. Branch `feat/benchmark-v03`. Scope: SPEC 3f (benchmark render-distance steps), item 8 (c) scene guard and camera, (a) shader advice, the `context` record, and the plan-review amendments E-M1, E-M2, E-M3 and E-L1. Plan: docs/v0.3/plans/ws-e.md.

## What changed

### 3f: every step measures its own terrain
- **Broadcast** (`client/benchmark/ClientKnobs`). After every render or simulation distance change the benchmark makes, including every restore, `Options.broadcastOptions()` sends the client information. `applyVanilla(..., save = false)` is unchanged, so options.txt is still written once, with the original values, when the run ends.
  - javap of `Options.broadcastOptions()` on 26.2 and 26.3: it returns at once when `minecraft.player` is null, so it's a no-op without a player (E-L1). `ClientPacketListener.broadcastClientInformation` only sends a `ClientInformation` that differs from the last one sent.
  - Simulation distance isn't in `ClientInformation`, so a broadcast after an SD-only change sends nothing. It's called anyway (AC8.1 says "RD/SD"); no claim is made that it changes anything on the server (E-L1).
  - A broadcast that throws is collected like any other apply failure, so the DH/Iris toggles of the same change still run, and the step (or restore) then reports the failure.
  - The seam is `ClientKnobs.Vanilla` (apply + broadcast); `ClientKnobsTest` counts the calls with a fake.
- **Chunk-presence settle** (`core/benchmark/SettleCheck`, E-M1).
  - The area is the circle dx² + dz² ≤ (RD − 1)² in chunks around the camera's chunk (377 chunks at RD 12, the research probe's number). The server sends at least that circle (`ChunkTrackingView.isWithinDistance` is a circle of radius RD with a 1-chunk buffer).
  - A step settles once the protocol's minimum has passed and, for 10 ticks in a row, no chunk of the area is missing on the client (`ClientChunkCache.hasChunk`) and the sections are built (Sodium's `isTerrainRenderComplete`, else vanilla's). Otherwise it ends at the timeout.
  - The radius is capped at the server's limit (`maxRenderDistance`: the remote server's view distance, or the option's range): chunks beyond it never arrive, so without the cap a Measure at RD 32 on a view-distance-10 server would time out on every step.
  - A step whose settle timed out with more than 2% of the area missing is recorded with `complete = false`. The render distance search never counts it as a pass (`RenderDistancePlanner.record(rd, stats, complete)`), and the best-effort fallback ignores it while any complete step exists. The result screen marks such a row "✘*" and explains it in one line.
  - Every step logs one line: `Benchmark settle <kind> <knobs>: p of n chunks within r present, client holds L, s s` (WARN with the missing count when incomplete). The measurement line also logs the client's chunk count.
- **Timeout** (E-M3). `BenchmarkController.Config.timeoutSeconds` was already the settle timeout; the default stays 20 s, and the 3f game test uses 60 s.
- **CURRENT-scene cap** (E-M2). `BenchmarkSession.maxRenderDistance(scene, startRd, limit)`: in the CURRENT scene Tune tests at most start RD + 8. The benchmark world keeps the full range. The menu shows "Higher render distances load and save more of this world." under the scene hint when the scene is CURRENT (worded to fit 320 GUI pixels). README says so in the benchmark paragraph.
  - "Join-time render distance" is read as the render distance when the Tune starts. The options screen saves, and so broadcasts, every change, so that's the distance the world was last loaded at.
- **Test hooks.** `Config` gains `targetFps` (a fixed target) and `maxRenderDistance` (a cap), with the old 4-argument constructor kept (RigTuneClientGameTest uses it). `BenchmarkController.setSweepListener` is called on the render thread just before a step records its first frame. `Outcome.settles()` lists each step's settle.
- **Dev target** (E-L1). `-Drigtune.dev.targetFps=<fps>` replaces the refresh-rate target (logged as a WARN). It's read when a run starts, so `JAVA_TOOL_OPTIONS=-Drigtune.dev.targetFps=300` also reaches `runProductionSmoke` without a build change.

### 8c: scene guard and camera
- `core/benchmark/SceneVariety` checks the terrain noise (floor height from `getBaseHeight(OCEAN_FLOOR_WG)`, biome from `getUncachedNoiseBiome` at the floor) every 16 blocks within 192 blocks of the camera (441 samples). It rejects "ocean" (> 50% below sea level within 128), "flat" (population sd < 8 or range < 24 within 128) and "monotone" (fewer than 2 biomes with ≥ 5% within 192). The unit test's fixture is the research probe's grid (src/test/resources/benchmark/scene-grid.csv, identical on 26.2 and 26.3) and reproduces the research's numbers: water 0%, sd 18.5, range 57, 4 biomes, 14.7% water within 192.
- BenchmarkGameTest samples it on the server thread and fails when the scene is rejected, on every leg, and logs the fingerprint: `floor 117 minecraft:forest; within 128: water 0.0%, height sd 18.5, range 57; within 192: 4 biomes >= 5%, water 14.7%`.
- **Camera** = terrain floor + 16 (`BenchmarkWorld.cameraY`): 133 on both versions. When the camera block or the one above isn't air (a very tall tree on a future version), it falls back to the v0.2 rule (10 above the highest motion-blocking block), never lower, with a WARN. The game test asserts y = floor + 16 and that both blocks are air.

### 8a: shader advice (read-only)
- `core/benchmark/ShaderAdvice.costPercent(cost, target)` is present exactly when a Tune measured the shader cost, the 1% low with shaders is below the target, without shaders it reaches the target, and the gap is at least 10%. The gap is the number shown: the share of the shaders-off 1% low the pack takes, 100 × (off − on) / off (16% for v0.2's MakeUp-UltraFast numbers, 502 → 601). The cost line above it shows the other direction (shaders off: +20%), so the line never shows a number below the 10% it was gated on.
- The result screen shows it as two lines under the shader cost line: "Your shader pack costs about N% of your 1% lows." and "A lighter profile in the pack's settings may reach your target." The wording is generic: the Iris menu path isn't verified (E-L1). Nothing is written to `iris.properties` or `shaderpacks/*.txt`.

### `context` on new runs
- `BenchmarkRecord.context` (optional, last component): `dhRendering`, `shaders` (the start knobs), `shaderPack` (`shaderPack` from `config/iris.properties`, only with shaders in use), `width`/`height` (`Window.getWidth/getHeight`, the framebuffer), `fullscreen` (`options.fullscreen()`, same API on 26.2 and 26.3), `protocol` 1. `schemaVersion` stays 1; a null context isn't written, so older runs are saved back byte-identical.
- 0.2.0 compatibility (AC8.8): 0.2.0's `BenchmarkHistory`, `BenchmarkRecord`, `BenchmarkMath` and `FrameStats` are pinned under `src/test/java/io/github/chaotix345/rigtune/v020/core/benchmark/` (`git show v0.2.0:<path>`, package line changed only). `src/test/resources/v020/benchmarks.json` was written by that pinned `save`. `BenchmarkCompatibilityTest`: the fixture loads with every field equal to the pinned reader's view and saves back byte-identical; the pinned reader loads a 0.3.0 file with `context` (not moved aside, every other field equal, pairs and chart intact); a 0.2.0 rewrite drops only `context`.

## For DESIGN.md (the coordinator folds these in)
- **Benchmark v2, step 2 (render distance):** replace "wait until the chunk sections have compiled (or a 20 s timeout)" with: "tell the server the new render distance (`Options.broadcastOptions()`, no options.txt write), then wait until every chunk within RD − 1 of the camera (a circle) is on the client and the sections have compiled, or a 20 s timeout; a step that timed out with more than 2% of those chunks missing never counts as meeting the target. In the player's own world Tune tests at most 8 above the starting render distance, because each tested distance makes the server load, generate and save that much more of the world; the benchmark menu says so."
- **Benchmark world:** the camera is at the terrain floor from the noise + 16 (y 133 on 26.2 and 26.3), no longer 10 above the highest motion-blocking block, which depended on a tree. A game test checks the scene with `SceneVariety` on every supported version and logs its fingerprint, so a worldgen change in a new Minecraft version is noticed.
- **Result screen:** the shader advice line (above), and each new run's `context` in benchmarks.json.
- **Correction to v0.2's note** (docs/v0.2/design/ws-c.md, "Camera spot": "26.2 and 26.3 generate different terrain for the same seed (surface 117 vs 123)"): the terrain doesn't differ. The noise (floor height and biome) is identical at all 9,409 samples within ±768 blocks (docs/research/v0.3/benchmark.md §2.5); only features such as trees are placed differently, and the 6-block difference was a tree canopy on the camera column on 26.3. The benchmark world is still recreated per Minecraft version, because the placed features differ.

## Deviations
- The shader advice is two display lines, not one (one line at the spec's length is clipped on a 427-pixel-wide GUI).
- The 10% gap is measured as the shaders' share of the shaders-off 1% low (the number the advice shows), a little stricter than the research's gain over the shaders-on 1% low (10% share = 11.1% gain).
- The chunk-count settle also applies to Measure and to the cost reports (any step), not only to Tune's render distance steps; only the render distance search uses the "incomplete" flag.
